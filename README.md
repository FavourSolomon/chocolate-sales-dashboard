# chocolate-sales-dashboard
An Excel dashboard analysing chocolate sales performance across products, countries, salespeople, and time.



Project Overview

This project explores chocolate sales performance using Microsoft Excel. The dashboard provides insights into sales amounts, boxes shipped, product performance, country performance, salesperson performance, and sales trends over time.

The main question guiding this analysis was: Does shipping more boxes of chocolate actually mean a higher sales amount?

Business Objectives

The objectives of this project were to:

- Analyse sales performance across different chocolate products.
- Compare boxes shipped with total sales amounts.
- Identify the best-performing countries and salespeople.
- Examine sales trends across months, quarters, and years.
- Present findings in an easy to understand dashboard.

Tools Used

- Microsoft Excel
- PivotTables
- Charts and dashboard visualisation

Dataset

The dataset contains chocolate sales records covering January 2022 to August 2024.

It includes information about:

- Sales Person
- Country
- Product 
- Date
- Amount 
- Boxes Shipped 

The dataset was already clean, so no additional data cleaning was required before the analysis.

Analysis Approach

I used Excel PivotTables and charts to summarise and compare sales performance across products, countries, salespeople, and time periods.

The dashboard brings these findings together to make the results easier to explore and interpret.

Key Insights

1. Boxes Shipped vs. Sales Amount

The 50% Dark Bite product recorded the highest number of boxes shipped, at 29,810. However, Smooth Silky Salty generated the highest total sales amount, at approximately $1.12 million.

This shows that the product with the highest shipment volume did not necessarily generate the highest sales amount.

2. Yearly Sales Performance

The dashboard recorded its highest total sales amount in 2024, at approximately $6.96 million.

The dataset covers January to August for each year from 2022 to 2024, allowing comparisons across the same eight-month period.

3. Quarterly Sales Trends

Q1 recorded the highest total sales amount in each year, while Q3 recorded the lowest.

However, Q3 contains data for July and August only, as the dataset ends in August. This should be considered when interpreting the quarterly results.

4. Country Performance

Australia recorded the highest total sales amount, at approximately $3.65 million, while New Zealand recorded the lowest, at approximately $3.04 million.

5. Salesperson Performance

Chesbonell recorded the highest total sales amount among the salespeople, at approximately $1.023 million.

Recommendations

Based on the findings, I recommend that the business:

- Compare product prices and sales volumes to understand why the most-shipped product did not generate the highest sales amount.
- Investigate the factors contributing to consistently strong Q1 performance.
- Examine differences in product mix, pricing, and shipment volumes across countries.
- Review the performance of top-performing salespeople to identify practices that could benefit the wider team.

Limitations

- The dataset covers January to August for each year from 2022 to 2024, so the analysis does not include complete calendar years.
- Q4 data is unavailable, limiting full-year quarterly comparisons.
- The dataset does not provide individual product prices or sufficient pricing details to explain the differences in sales amounts across products.
- The analysis identifies sales patterns but does not establish the causes of those patterns.
- The dataset was already clean, so the project focused on analysis and visualisation rather than data cleaning.

Conclusion

This project demonstrates how Microsoft Excel can be used to analyse sales data and present business insights through a dashboard.

One of the key findings was that the product with the highest number of boxes shipped was not the product with the highest total sales amount. This highlights the importance of examining multiple performance measures rather than relying on shipment volume alone.

The project strengthened my practical skills in Excel analysis, PivotTables, data visualisation, and communicating findings in a business context.
